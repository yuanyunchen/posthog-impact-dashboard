One main chain, two side channels: rules filter, a model grades, Claude judges

  • Rules see everyone for $0; Flash grades all 16k PRs for $5.62
  • Claude only ever sees 22 people
  • Side channels: an audit for rule blind spots, a graph for team position
Details

"Flash" is a low-cost DeepSeek model: about $0.00034 per triage and $0.0015 per deep read. Claude runs as agent sessions and is not billed per call. The $7.35 total covers every cached DeepSeek call, development runs included.

Details
Does high approval-network centrality mean a trusted gatekeeper?
No. It means receiving approvals from central reviewers. fuziontech gave 912 approvals but wrote 9 PRs, so he ranks 139th of 200 on it. He is the hub of a 15-person review community, which is a different kind of leverage.
Can the company brief mislead?
Yes. Warehouse sources alone holds 18.7% of human PRs. The decider was told to judge whether that is important work or templated breadth, and it concluded much of it is templated catalogue additions (about 870 add-source and add-table PRs).
  • Top bridges by betweenness: rafaeelaudibert (reaches 12 communities), gantoine (13), Gilbert09 (11). 9 of the top 10 bridges are among the 22 finalists. Most later authors of the same files: Gilbert09 and webjunkie, 106 each, a proxy for shared code rather than proof of adoption.
  • Single points of approval (one person giving ≥80% of ≥5 approvals in an area): none found in the full run, not yet verified whether that is real or a strict threshold.
  • Review requests are only known for the 2,905 PRs whose review details were fetched; elsewhere they count as unknown, never as 0.
  • Follow-up fix: a fix PR within 14 days that explicitly cites the original, excluding "unrelated / flake / see also" sentences. Later authors of the same files: a later change to a source file he edited by ≥20 lines; files touched by ≥25 people are ignored.
  • Communities: Louvain on the review graph. Person-level metrics with fewer than 5 samples are unknown.

③ Roles: abstract upward from PRs to "who is this person"

  • One-to-many: 1 decision → 22 reports → 3–8 workstreams each
  • Workstreams are clustered by script, removing one retelling
  • Analysts never see the reference score

Five controls against information loss between layers

  • Analysts: a fixed report plus at least 2 questions of their own
  • They check 4 metrics against the 22 before grading
  • When behaviour and PR text disagree, behaviour wins

Fixed report: one-line positioning, 2–3 core contributions, team position, four 1–10 grades (9–10 company-critical · 7–8 team pillar · 5–6 steady · 3–4 local · 1–2 marginal), red flags and counter-evidence, coverage.

Own questions: for example "is the work bunched around one launch?", "how good is the AI-assisted output?", "did he pick up problems others had dropped?". Each states why, which queries ran, what was found and how it moved a grade.

④ Multiple angles: scores set the scale, evidence-backed arguments decide

Each tier doubles the points; splitting one change earns only a quarter per extra PR

  • Tiers reflect impact, not size of the change
  • "Major" requires a verbatim quote from the PR body
  • Two majors in one episode score 10, not 16

Four angles, each checked by three kinds of evidence

  • Delivery carries half the weight; quality and team can still sink a case
  • Missing data is skipped and shown as coverage
  • R is computed after the analysts grade, to avoid circularity

Three rules were corrected during the run, and now hold by construction

  • A rank among near-identical rates is noise, not a red flag
  • Both decisions must list a finalist in their top 7, or justify
  • Any rank more than 3 away from R must be explained
Details
  • Consensus: Borda over the two decisions (rank 1 = 10 points … rank 10 = 1); ties go to the higher R. Head-to-head only for pairs in consensus ranks 4–8 that the two decisions order differently; in round 2 only rafaeelaudibert vs Piccirello, won by rafaeelaudibert in both orders.
  • Devil's advocate: for each of the 5 it wrote the strongest objection and a likely replacement. Gilbert09 was kept without a re-test (the objection rated itself weak). Four challenges were tested head to head in both orders: webjunkie beat haacked and pauldambra beat tatoalo; haacked beat Piccirello, so they swapped; mayteio beat rafaeelaudibert, but the second decision ranked him 12th, so gate (c) blocked the swap.
  • Gates, all three must pass: (a) ≥2 major PRs, or ≥1 landmark, or top 3 in team leverage; (b) fewer than 3 reverts, and not a follow-up-fix rate that is both in the worst 20% and ≥8%; (c) both decisions place him in their top 7. Failing without a written case means the next person moves up.
  • Stability = share of 1,000 weightings in which R puts him in the top 5: each weight × U(0.5, 1.5), renormalised; ≥70% stable, 40–70% borderline, below 40% verdict-only, which needs a written justification.
  • The two decisions agree closely overall: Kendall τ = 0.922 across all 22.
  • Impact points P = 2^(4ℓ/3), where ℓ (0–3) weights user value, criticality and novelty from the deep read; same scale as tiers. A quote not found in the source costs a point.
